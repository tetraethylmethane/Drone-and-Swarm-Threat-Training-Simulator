## Unresolved questions (need direct verification)

1. **Althimis:** confirm the legal name ("Althimis Defense" or "Althimis Inc."), headquarters, and whether Guardian is commercially available or still a demonstrator. Read the EIN Presswire release and the NextGen Defense article directly.
2. **DefenceVR KAVACH-SIM:** what "after-action-review analytics" measures; whether scoring rules are visible to instructors; whether sessions can be replayed or re-scored; how threat scenarios are authored.
3. **VR Astra:** what "adaptive scoring" and "AI Assessment" do, and whether results can be audited or exported. Clarify what the "from ₹2.5L" price includes.
4. **Zen Technologies:** whether a counter-drone *training* mode exists for the Anti Drone System, and whether Zen UAV Sim has an instructor station and debrief.
5. **MAK:** AAR or replay tooling for the FPV and counter-drone configuration (MAK's wider product range may provide it; not stated on the page read).
6. **Bagira:** UAV Mission Simulator scope and assessment features (product page not read).
7. **All companies:** any published training-effectiveness study. None was found on the pages read.
8. **Big Bang Boom:** the "15+" and "7+" countries figures conflict on one page. Treat both as unverified.

## Limits of this research

- Only public web pages were read: no brochures, demos, procurement records or user interviews.
- A capability marked `Unknown` may exist. Only direct contact or a demonstration can settle it.
- Vendor sites describe themselves favourably, and secondary sources can repeat press releases.
- Maturity labels ("emerging", "established") are judgements from public signals (portfolio
  breadth, investor material, deployment claims), not audited facts.

Effect on [competitor-landscape.md](competitor-landscape.md) and the roadmap:
- DefenceVR KAVACH-SIM now has a **claimed** adaptive difficulty feature, so Drikr's adaptive
  difficulty is not unique *as a claim*. What remains distinctive, on the evidence available, is
  transparency: recorded reasons, bounds, instructor override and reproducibility. This rests on the
  absence of public detail from the competitor, not on proof that the competitor lacks these
  features.
- Zen's air-defence simulator includes drone threats with an AAR. Zen is therefore a competitor
  for drone-threat *engagement* training, not for recognition and decision assessment.
